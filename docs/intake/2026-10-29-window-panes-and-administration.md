## Industrial window family

**Source:** Farmyard **`0ef5b7186970a32397a0c61bdedca2ba24c17c79`**, published `codex/window-family`. Principal files are `sites/tactical-animal/dist/art/windows/{catalog.js,catalog.json,geometry.js}`, `docs/art/windows/README.md` and `sites/tactical-animal/tests/window-art.test.mjs`. The three definition/factory/catalog files have identical Git blobs in the later runtime contribution, despite cherry-pick commit IDs differing.

There are **36 definitions**: full-height/waist-up × 1/2/3 m spans × six pane patterns, with three frame and three lower-wall finishes. Pane IDs are stable bottom-up `r0c0` etc. A clear 3 m span is one sheet, not three panes created by tile seams. Frames, mullions, sill and lower wall remain separate solids. Hiding a pane removes its glass and reflection together, preserving the structure. Joins halve touching posts without changing pane IDs.

**Accuracy:** Prefer true independent apertures and structural volumes over a painted opaque window or a whole-tile disappearance. A shot through glass, a mullion strike and a waist wall have different geometry. Keep actual local pane bounds and transformed solid matrices; a world AABB alone is not a rotated fracture shape. Existing walls stay 2 m high/.16 m thick, while this family's frame depth is .20 m. Clip the inherited 1.12 m wall overhang deliberately; do not stretch the new asset to hide a seam.

**Efficiency:** Code-based preference: immutable cached definitions and shared geometry/material resources are suitable for repeated windows. Finer divisions add hit candidates and visible pieces compared with a flat paint; no frame-time comparison was made. Prefer the simple clear pattern where individual cells add no game value, and divided patterns where impacts/readability matter. Keep catalog data in the headless contract and rendering/material generation in an optional provider using Nautilus's existing Three namespace.

**Narrative:** Subjectively, the inspected intact/one-pane-missing comparison makes damage location readable while retaining a convincing frame. Prefer this family for industrial buildings and retain the separate period sash/sculpture family for houses. The Administration Building's arched glazing is not a reason to transplant rectangular industrial frames into its historical facade.

**Proposed acceptance:** Coder/Editor review definition IDs, join cache keys, units, orientation, stable instance IDs, source closure and renderer isolation. Artist reviews front/back, tint/reflections, joins and full/waist apertures at intended game distance. UI supports pattern/finish selection and nonmutating intact/missing-pane previews. Confirm rotations, seam-spanning instances and no identity changes on save/restore. Never equate a selectable catalog entry with live collision or damage support.

## Saved pane damage and smashed-glass presentation

**Source:** Farmyard **`68c10c47b7cf5c47759ac2307f8f10c4c7eb6bab`**, published `codex/window-pane-integration`. Read `docs/window-integration-contract.md`, `docs/reviews/window-pane-integration/README.md`, `sites/tactical-animal/window-shatter-provenance.json` and `dist/{window-system.js,window-catalogue.js,window-shatter.js,window-view.js,window-authoring.js,window-editor.js}`. Its combined-review record explicitly leaves native WebGL, live field/editor controls and audible playback pending before donor promotion.

Farmyard stores broken pane IDs per stable window instance. Only accepted simulation impacts create break events. The first accepted hit breaks the pane and stops that round; later burst rounds can pass through the resulting hole. Mullions/sills/waist walls retain collision. Clear glass is transparent to sight and blocks flame until broken. Breaking it does not add walking/vaulting. Saved damage survives leaving/revisiting; transient shards/audio are not replayed on restore. Cosmetic event seeds derive from instance/pane/sequence, without consuming combat RNG.

**Accuracy:** Prefer pane-specific saved state and geometry over whole-window removal or cosmetic-only cracks. The first-hit-stops rule is a tactical abstraction, not validated projectile penetration physics. The fracture/bounce is tuned analytic presentation: its inspected gravity parameter is 7.8, it ejects fragments beyond the frame, and fragments do not hit props or cause damage. Favor preserving this explicit compatibility profile for Farmyard. Tinderbox should retain ownership of heat damage, flame transmission and any later thermal-fracture model; the current contribution adds no heat-break rule. Do not call the shared visual effect a fire/material simulation.

**Efficiency:** Prefer optional analytic shards over mandatory rigid-body simulation for ordinary tactical play. Source limits are six active meshes, 210 shared triangles, at most 70 per pane and three audio voices; simultaneous breaks share the budget. Effects last 2.2 s and can be suppressed for reduced motion, residency or budget without changing authoritative damage. These are verified limits/code behavior, not CPU/GPU or memory benchmarks. Keep a richer debris/heat policy optional if a game's central activity justifies its cost.

**Narrative:** Subjectively, localized missing panes, subsequent shots through a hole and a once-only crash communicate a persistent event better than a whole-window swap. Prefer the bounded effect as default tactical feedback, with reliable static broken state when motion/audio is disabled or unavailable. A silent restore should preserve the hole rather than retell the break. For Tinderbox, flame/glass behavior should tell the same story as its authoritative fire state.

**Proposed interface/review:** Share definition geometry, versioned instance/pane damage and confirmed event receipts; put rendering, audio and game impact policy behind adapters. The donor's `farmyardWindows` portable extension and AP/combat/save namespace are Farmyard-specific, not an automatic Nautilus schema choice. The architect should define a namespaced shared capability with explicit unsupported-feature rejection, preserving original map/block formats and existing identities. Keep costs/turns/projectiles/inventory/world queries consumer-owned.

Coder checks idempotent breaks/events, transactional damage, exact contacts versus rounded tile picking, stale snapshots, replay/save behavior, ray/solid heights, burst invalidation and separate random streams. Editor/UI check placement/removal restores replaced walls, undo/redo and JSON round trips, invalid/overlapping definitions, capability rejection and real browser picking. Artist checks source pane bounds, tint removal and fracture clearance. Audio needs a gesture/mute and stale decode cancellation; failed loading must not block combat.

The source supports flat receiving floors at world height 0 and quarter-turn placements. Reject upper floors, slopes, unsupported floor heights and arbitrary angled joins until separately reviewed. Do not generalize to Nautilus's six floors or free-angle wall runs by stretching the effect. Preserve source study **`ebf51393909040b61a7c40649d9d5f0ac3678735`**, its adaptation record, the unchanged recording and its CC0 credit/license. The original study-code license is unspecified; the audio's license is not a license for all source/art.

## Administration Building

**Source:** Tinderbox **`43337a33ddfe4dff6849654c827c56e1f24e1acc`**, published `codex/administration-building-art`, based on the previously reported New York branch. This is a **new Administration Building**, distinct from the already deferred New York State Building. It reuses genuine pinned `wallPanels`, `measureWallRun` and `wallRunErrors` through **five local native tiers**. That is useful module reuse, not a claim that one map supports the landmark's complete height. Runtime dependencies include the existing New York `forms.js` and the old Nautilus `90054d...` wall helper; reconcile to the current shared API rather than vendoring another engine inside Nautilus.

**Accuracy:** Prefer explicit native construction, clipped floor cells, open rotunda and circular-stair geometry over a rescaled facade/envelope. The donor records an approximately 79.77 m plan reading with uncertainty, authored at 80 m; the crown is 76.32 m versus the guide's recorded 76.20 m. These are attributed source readings/adaptations, not this scout's historical measurements. The 40.6 m external dome deliberately differs from the source's nominal 36.576 m diameter. Rear/upper partitions, symmetry, fine sculpture and colours have documented uncertainty. Preserve those labels; native-grid conformance does not establish an exact reconstruction or structural simulation.

Physical structure/finish remain unassigned. The adapter's `wood` value is a geometry-validator token, not evidence that this building should burn like timber. Appearance swapping must not assign fuel or alter collision. The 288 visible stair treads and floor holes do not establish character navigation, railing collision or access to upper galleries.

**Efficiency:** Independently reproduced geometry counts: **614,274 triangles, 412 meshes, 229 instance batches and 20,441 floor tiles**. Native panels and tiles share geometry, but this is a large optional asset; no city-scale, GPU or memory benchmark was run. Prefer a separately loaded visual landmark with an exterior/LOD profile for initial inspection. Keep the complete architectural assembly as the richer reference. Do not make its interior/ornament cost mandatory for tactical maps, and do not call native panel reuse a demonstrated speed gain.

**Narrative:** Subjectively, the inspected actual-model CPU render has a distinctive gilded-dome silhouette suitable for an Exposition centerpiece. Prefer preserving that recognizable massing and source notes over squeezing it to ordinary house scale. Circular stairs and cut-floor algorithms may also be useful separate architectural contributions, even if full-landmark adoption waits.

**Adoption gate:** Body is 80 × 80 m; approaches/ornament extend about 92 × 92 m. Occupied surfaces include levels 7, 9, 19 and 23, beyond the six-layer map contract; level 36 marks the crown, not an occupied floor. The architect should choose a separately named visual-landmark contract or an explicit versioned height/assembly feature before map placement. A 24 m block or ordinary bounded flat consumer must not truncate, silently rescale or claim traversal of it. Reuse the New York height/performance decision as a prerequisite, without merging the two asset identities.

Proposed work: Coder reviews tier offsets/current API compatibility, bounds, packages, rotations and owned/borrowed resources; Artist reviews native/source dimensions, floor cuts/rotunda/stair geometry and a sensible simpler visual profile; Editor/UI provide optional inspection with clear height/integration status. Keep gameplay placement, collision, fire/collapse, stairs and physical materials for separate consumer contracts. This is source review work, not an instruction to redraw the building.
