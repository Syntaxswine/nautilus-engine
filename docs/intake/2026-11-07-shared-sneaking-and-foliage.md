## Design preference

- **Accuracy.** Prefer AFT's movement, hearing and exposure-driven awareness for stealth-oriented tactical play. It represents the separate effects of quieter steps, body exposure, posture, cover depth and lighting. Use distinct geometry results for concealment, movement and stopping a projectile. Open woodland admits paths and gaps, dense thicket can obscure vision and block walking, and leaves and wood have different projectile behavior. Preserve the wetland and bog distinctions. AFT's distances and rates are game abstractions without physical calibration.
- **Efficiency.** Farmyard's range multiplier is cheaper in isolation by code inspection. AFT adds sight rays, a cover-depth walk and awareness state per relevant observer/target pair. Use a shared foliage index tied to map revisions, early range/facing checks and boolean occlusion where a hit position is unnecessary. Reuse current scene queries and invalidate them when the authored patch changes. Verify boundary and nearest-hit parity before taking the newer AFT traversal. Measure sparse and dense cover in a representative scene, including Farmyard's lower-power target. This is an estimate of work and scaling. No CPU or memory benchmark was run.
- **Narrative.** Prefer the AFT tradeoff for AFT and comparable Farmyard modes because moving slowly through cover creates a readable chance to slip past a guard. Players should be able to distinguish brush that hides them from material that stops a shot. This is a subjective design choice. Preserve Farmyard's current outcomes for legacy saves until a mode explicitly adopts the shared profile. Tinderbox should reuse the same authored geometry and movement/obstruction meaning. Its fire materials and any stealth-specific experience should follow the needs of its fire scenarios.

Run the four Nautilus gates, source fixture regressions, the installed-package check and a focused visual review of the playable editor flow.
